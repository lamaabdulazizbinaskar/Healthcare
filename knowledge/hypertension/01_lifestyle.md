# Hypertension – lifestyle (non-drug) recommendations

All entries: Whelton PK, Carey RM, et al. 2017 ACC/AHA/AAPA/ABC/ACPM/AGS/APhA/ASH/ASPC/NMA/PCNA
Guideline for the Prevention, Detection, Evaluation, and Management of High Blood Pressure in
Adults. J Am Coll Cardiol 2018;71:e127–e248. Paraphrased by the LifeStep team (not quoted).

## HTN-SODIUM-01: Eat less sodium (salt)
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Section 6.2 recommendation 3 (COR I, LOE A); Table 15 (dietary sodium)
- tags: diet, sodium, salt, food
- frequency_hint: daily

Cutting sodium is recommended for adults with high blood pressure. The best goal is under
1500 mg of sodium a day, but lowering it by at least 1000 mg a day already helps most adults.
In people with hypertension this lowers systolic blood pressure by about 5 to 6 mmHg.

## HTN-SODIUM-03: Practical ways to cut salt
- source: Team-authored practical steps applying 2017 ACC/AHA Section 6.2 rec. 3 and ADA Standards of Care 2026 rec. 5.20 (limit processed foods to lower sodium)
- tags: diet, sodium, salt, cooking, food, processed
- frequency_hint: daily

Most sodium comes from processed and ready-made food. Helpful steps: don't add salt at the
table, use less salt and stock cubes when cooking, flavour food with spices, lemon or garlic,
and choose fresh rather than processed foods.

## HTN-SODIUM-04: Common salty foods in Saudi homes
- source: Team-authored local examples applying 2017 ACC/AHA Table 15 (sodium) and ADA 2026 rec. 5.20 (requires dietitian review)
- tags: diet, sodium, salt, saudi, local, food, kabsa, stock cube, cheese
- frequency_hint: weekly

Often salty: stock cubes and seasoning powder in kabsa and soups, white and processed cheese,
pickles and olives, ready-made bread, processed meats, salted nuts and chips, fast food.
Swaps: half a stock cube or none with more spices (cumin, black lime, cardamom), small portions
of pickles, unsalted nuts.

## HTN-DASH-01: Follow a DASH eating pattern
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Section 6.2 recommendation 2 (COR I, LOE A); Table 15 (DASH dietary pattern)
- tags: diet, dash, vegetables, fruit, whole grains, dairy, food
- frequency_hint: daily

A heart-healthy diet such as DASH is recommended. DASH is rich in fruits, vegetables, whole
grains and low-fat dairy, with less saturated and total fat. In people with hypertension it
lowers systolic blood pressure by about 11 mmHg.

## HTN-DASH-02: Vegetables and fruit at meals
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Table 15 (DASH: diet rich in fruits and vegetables)
- tags: diet, dash, vegetables, fruit, food
- frequency_hint: daily

The DASH pattern is rich in vegetables and fruit. Adding vegetables or fruit to meals is a
simple way to follow it.

## HTN-DASH-03: Whole grains, low-fat dairy, less saturated fat
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Table 15 (DASH: whole grains, low-fat dairy, reduced saturated and total fat)
- tags: diet, dash, whole grains, dairy, fat, food, rice, bread
- frequency_hint: weekly

Choose whole grains instead of refined grains, low-fat dairy, and fewer foods high in
saturated fat (fatty meat, ghee, butter).

## HTN-POTASSIUM-01: Potassium from food
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Section 6.2 recommendation 4 (COR I, LOE A); Table 15 (dietary potassium)
- tags: diet, potassium, fruit, vegetables, food
- frequency_hint: daily
- contraindications: chronic kidney disease, or drugs that reduce potassium excretion (e.g. ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium-sparing diuretics)

More potassium, preferably from food, is recommended unless the person has chronic kidney
disease or takes drugs that reduce potassium excretion. Target 3500–5000 mg/day; about
4–5 mmHg lower systolic BP. Potassium-rich foods include bananas, oranges, tomatoes, potatoes,
leafy greens, beans and yogurt.

## HTN-ACTIVITY-01: Aerobic activity
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Section 6.2 recommendation 5 (COR I, LOE A); Table 15 (aerobic)
- tags: activity, exercise, aerobic, walking, minutes per week
- frequency_hint: weekly

More physical activity with a structured exercise program is recommended. Aerobic target:
90–150 minutes a week (moderate, 65–75% of heart-rate reserve); about 5–8 mmHg lower systolic BP.

## HTN-ACTIVITY-05: Handgrip (isometric) exercise
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Table 15 (isometric resistance)
- tags: activity, exercise, isometric, handgrip, seated
- frequency_hint: weekly

Handgrip 4 × 2 minutes with 1 minute rest between, at 30–40% of maximum effort, 3 sessions a
week for 8–10 weeks; about 5 mmHg lower systolic BP. It can be done sitting down.

## HTN-ACTIVITY-06: Dynamic resistance (strength) exercise
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Table 15 (dynamic resistance)
- tags: activity, exercise, strength, resistance
- frequency_hint: weekly

90–150 minutes a week; 6 exercises, 3 sets each, 10 repetitions per set; about 4 mmHg lower
systolic BP.

## HTN-ACTIVITY-03: Adapting activity to pain or mobility limits
- source: Team-authored adaptation of 2017 ACC/AHA Section 6.2 rec. 5 / Table 15 (handgrip is done seated); for people with diabetes see ADA 2026 Section 5 "Pre-exercise Risk" (S104) and rec. 5.38. Needs clinician review.
- tags: activity, limitations, knee pain, seated, elderly, mobility, balance
- frequency_hint: daily

When knee, hip or back pain or poor balance make walking hard, activity can be done seated or
holding a stable chair (seated marching, arm raises, handgrip). Build up slowly and stop with
chest pain, dizziness or severe shortness of breath.

## HTN-WEIGHT-01: Lose weight if overweight
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Section 6.2 recommendation 1 (COR I, LOE A); Table 15 (weight loss)
- tags: weight, overweight, bmi
- frequency_hint: monthly
- contraindications: only when overweight (BMI ≥ 25)

Weight loss is recommended for adults with high BP who are overweight. Best goal is ideal
body weight, but aim for at least 1 kg; expect about 1 mmHg lower BP per kg lost.

## HTN-WEIGHT-02: Check your weight regularly
- source: Team-authored self-monitoring step supporting 2017 ACC/AHA Section 6.2 rec. 1
- tags: weight, monitoring
- frequency_hint: weekly
- contraindications: only when overweight

Weighing yourself on the same day each week helps you see progress towards a weight goal.

## HTN-ALCOHOL-01: Limit alcohol if consumed
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Section 6.2 recommendation 6 (COR I, LOE A); Table 15
- tags: alcohol
- frequency_hint: daily
- contraindications: only if the patient drinks alcohol

Adults who drink should have no more than 2 drinks a day (men) or 1 (women); about 4 mmHg lower systolic BP.

## HTN-CAFFEINE-01: No caffeine, exercise or smoking 30 minutes before measuring
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Table 8 (checklist for accurate BP measurement, step 1) and Table 10 (HBPM procedures); Table 14 (caffeine: generally < 300 mg/day)
- tags: caffeine, coffee, tea, qahwa, measurement
- frequency_hint: daily

Avoid caffeine, exercise and smoking for at least 30 minutes before measuring. Coffee causes
a short rise in BP; caffeine is generally limited to under 300 mg a day.

## HTN-TOBACCO-01: Stop smoking
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Table 5 (current smoking and secondhand smoke are modifiable CVD risk factors); ADA Standards of Care 2026 rec. 5.40 (advise complete avoidance of tobacco and vaping)
- tags: smoking, tobacco, shisha, vape
- frequency_hint: daily
- contraindications: only if the patient smokes or vapes

Smoking (and second-hand smoke) raises cardiovascular risk in people with high blood
pressure. Complete avoidance of tobacco and vaping is advised; counselling and medicines help.
