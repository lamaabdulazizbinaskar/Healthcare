# Hypertension – home monitoring, medicines, safety

Source: 2017 ACC/AHA High Blood Pressure Guideline (J Am Coll Cardiol 2018;71:e127–e248).
Paraphrased by the LifeStep team.

## HTN-HBPM-01: Measure blood pressure at home with a validated device
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Section 4.2 (out-of-office and self-monitoring of BP); Table 10 (use automated validated devices, preferably with memory)
- tags: monitoring, home bp, device, monitor
- frequency_hint: weekly

Home blood pressure monitoring is recommended to confirm and manage hypertension. Use an
automated, validated device with the right cuff size; devices that store readings are preferred.

## HTN-HBPM-02: How to take a correct home reading
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Table 10 (procedures for home BP monitoring) and Table 8 (checklist for accurate measurement)
- tags: monitoring, home bp, measurement, technique, morning
- frequency_hint: daily

No smoking, caffeine or exercise for 30 minutes before. Rest quietly for 5 minutes. Sit on a
straight-backed chair, back supported, feet flat, legs uncrossed, arm on a table with the upper
arm at heart level. Take at least 2 readings 1 minute apart in the morning before medicines and
in the evening before supper.

## HTN-HBPM-03: Keep your readings and share them
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Table 10 (record readings; monitors with memory preferred; weekly readings before clinic visits)
- tags: monitoring, log, doctor visit, report
- frequency_hint: monthly

Record readings (or use a monitor with memory) and bring them to your visits so your doctor
can adjust treatment.

## HTN-MEDS-01: Take blood pressure medicines as prescribed
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Section 12.1 (adherence strategies) and 12.1.1 (once-daily dosing and combination pills improve adherence)
- tags: medication, adherence, pills
- frequency_hint: daily

Not taking medicines as prescribed is a major reason BP stays uncontrolled. Take them every
day, even when you feel well; never stop or change a dose without your doctor.

## HTN-MEDS-02: Habits that help you remember
- source: Team-authored habits supporting 2017 ACC/AHA Section 12.1 (adherence); simplified once-daily routines per 12.1.1
- tags: medication, adherence, reminders, routine
- frequency_hint: daily

Link medicines to a daily habit (e.g. breakfast), keep them where you will see them, use a
weekly pill box or phone alarm.

## HTN-MEDS-03: Refill before you run out
- source: Team-authored step based on 2017 ACC/AHA Section 12.1.1 synopsis (patients often lack medicine supply)
- tags: medication, refill, pharmacy
- frequency_hint: monthly

Check how many tablets are left and arrange a refill in time.

## HTN-WARN-01: Very high BP with symptoms is an emergency
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Section 11.2 (hypertensive crises: BP > 180/120 with new or worsening target-organ damage)
- tags: safety, crisis, emergency
- frequency_hint: daily

BP above 180/120 with symptoms such as chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache,
confusion, vision changes, weakness or numbness can be an emergency: call 997.

## HTN-SLEEP-02: Snoring and pauses in breathing
- source: 2017 ACC/AHA, Section 5.4.4 and Table 5 (obstructive sleep apnoea is a cause/risk factor of hypertension); ADA 2026 rec. 5.56 (screen for sleep disorders in diabetes)
- tags: sleep, apnoea, snoring, doctor
- frequency_hint: monthly

Loud snoring, daytime sleepiness or pauses in breathing can be signs of obstructive sleep
apnoea, which is linked to high blood pressure. Ask your doctor; this is not a diagnosis.
