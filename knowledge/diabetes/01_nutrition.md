# Diabetes – nutrition

All entries: American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee. Standards of Care in
Diabetes—2026. Diabetes Care 2026;49(Suppl. 1). Section 5 (S89–S131). Paraphrased by the
Khutwa team; ADA text is not reproduced (ADA license prohibits reproduction and text/data mining).

## DM-NUTR-01: A healthy eating pattern
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.14 (B) and rec. 5.13 (B)
- tags: diet, food, vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, nuts, eating pattern
- frequency_hint: daily

Build meals around non-starchy vegetables, whole fruits, legumes (beans, lentils, chickpeas),
lean protein, whole grains, nuts and seeds, and low-fat dairy. Eat less red meat, sugary
drinks, sweets, refined grains (white bread, white rice) and processed foods. Meal plans should
be individual.

## DM-NUTR-02: High-fibre, less processed carbohydrates
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.24 (B) (≥ 14 g fibre per 1000 kcal) and rec. 5.15 (B) (reducing carbohydrate can improve glucose, e.g. by limiting processed foods)
- tags: diet, carbohydrate, fibre, whole grains, rice, bread, sugar
- frequency_hint: daily

Choose minimally processed, high-fibre carbohydrate foods (whole grains, legumes, vegetables,
whole fruit). Some adults improve their glucose by eating less carbohydrate, and an easy way is
to limit processed foods.

## DM-NUTR-03: Water instead of sugary drinks and juice
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.21 (A) and rec. 5.25 (B)
- tags: drinks, water, juice, soda, sugar, sweet tea
- frequency_hint: daily

Choose water over other drinks. Replace sugar-sweetened drinks, including all juices, with
water or low/no-calorie drinks, and keep foods with added sugar to a minimum.

## DM-NUTR-04: Limit sodium
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.20 (B)
- tags: diet, sodium, salt, processed food
- frequency_hint: daily

People with diabetes should keep sodium under 2300 mg a day when appropriate; the best way is
eating fewer processed foods. (With high blood pressure, the ACC/AHA goal is lower: < 1500 mg.)

## DM-NUTR-05: More plant protein
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.29 (B)
- tags: diet, protein, legumes, nuts, seeds, beans, lentils
- frequency_hint: weekly

Include more plant protein such as nuts, seeds and legumes to lower cardiovascular risk.

## DM-NUTR-06: Mediterranean-style choices
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.30 (A/B)
- tags: diet, fish, nuts, seeds, olive oil, mediterranean
- frequency_hint: weekly

A Mediterranean-style pattern, rich in fatty fish, nuts and seeds, lowers cardiovascular risk
and improves glucose metabolism.

## DM-NUTR-07: Limit saturated fat
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.31 (B)
- tags: diet, fat, ghee, butter, fatty meat
- frequency_hint: weekly

Limit foods high in saturated fat (fatty meat, ghee, butter, full-fat cheese) to lower
cardiovascular risk.

## DM-NUTR-08: Steady carbohydrate timing with fixed insulin doses
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.28 (B)
- tags: insulin, carbohydrate, meal timing, hypoglycemia
- frequency_hint: daily
- contraindications: only for people using fixed insulin doses

People on fixed insulin doses do best with consistent carbohydrate amounts at consistent times,
which improves glucose and lowers the risk of low blood sugar.

## DM-NUTR-09: Supplements don't lower glucose
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.16 (C)
- tags: supplements, cinnamon, herbs, vitamins, aloe, chromium, magnesium
- frequency_hint: monthly

Vitamins, minerals (e.g. magnesium, chromium) and herbs or spices (e.g. cinnamon, aloe vera)
are not recommended for lowering blood glucose. Tell your care team what supplements you use.

## DM-NUTR-10: Sweeteners in moderation
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.22 (B)
- tags: sweeteners, sugar, tea, coffee
- frequency_hint: weekly

Non-nutritive sweeteners can replace sugar in moderation and for the short term to cut
calories and carbohydrate.

## DM-ALCOHOL-01: Alcohol
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.18 (B) and rec. 5.19 (B)
- tags: alcohol, hypoglycemia
- frequency_hint: daily
- contraindications: only if the patient drinks alcohol

People who don't drink should not start. Those who drink should stay within daily limits and,
if on insulin or insulin secretagogues, watch for delayed low blood sugar after drinking.

## DM-WEIGHT-01: Weight loss of 5–7% if overweight
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.12 (A); rec. 13.12 (A) for older adults who can exercise safely
- tags: weight, overweight, obesity, bmi
- frequency_hint: monthly
- contraindications: only when overweight (BMI ≥ 25)

For people with overweight or obesity, a plan combining food, activity and behaviour support
aims for at least 5–7% weight loss.

## DM-OLD-01: Enough protein for older adults
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 13.11a (B)
- tags: older adults, protein, elderly, muscle
- frequency_hint: daily
- contraindications: age ≥ 65

Older adults with diabetes need healthy eating with enough protein (at least 0.8 g per kg body
weight per day) to keep muscle and strength.

## DM-NUTR-11: The diabetes plate method
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, Section 5 "Eating/Meal Plan Approaches and Methods" (S94): the diabetes plate method is a simple visual approach to portioning (half the plate non-starchy vegetables) and improved A1C in a trial
- tags: plate, portion, meal planning, vegetables, protein, carbohydrate, rice
- frequency_hint: daily

A simple way to build a meal: fill half the plate with non-starchy vegetables, a quarter with
protein, and a quarter with carbohydrate foods (preferably whole grains or legumes).
