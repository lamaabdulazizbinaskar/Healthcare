# Diabetes – activity, glucose safety, self-care

Source: ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026 (Diabetes Care 2026;49(Suppl. 1)). Paraphrased.

## DM-ACT-01: 150 minutes of activity a week
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.36 (B for type 2 / C for type 1)
- tags: activity, exercise, aerobic, walking, minutes per week
- frequency_hint: weekly

Most adults with diabetes should do 150 minutes or more of moderate-to-vigorous aerobic
activity a week, over at least 3 days, with no more than 2 days in a row without activity.

## DM-ACT-02: Strength exercise 2–3 times a week
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.37 (B/C)
- tags: activity, strength, resistance, muscle
- frequency_hint: weekly

Do 2–3 resistance (strength) sessions a week on non-consecutive days.

## DM-ACT-03: Break up sitting every 30 minutes
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.34 (B; sitting C)
- tags: activity, sitting, sedentary, movement
- frequency_hint: daily

Long sitting should be interrupted at least every 30 minutes; it helps blood glucose. If you
are below the activity goal, increase activity step by step from where you are now.

## DM-ACT-04: Balance and flexibility for older adults
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.38 (C); rec. 13.11b (B)
- tags: activity, balance, flexibility, older adults, elderly, falls
- frequency_hint: weekly

Older adults with diabetes should do flexibility and balance training 2–3 times a week, plus
regular activity and strength work as tolerated.

## DM-ACT-05: Check safety before starting harder exercise
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, Section 5 "Pre-exercise Risk and Baseline Physical Activity" (S104)
- tags: activity, safety, doctor, chest pain, retinopathy, neuropathy, foot ulcer, dizziness
- frequency_hint: daily

People at high risk should start with short, low-intensity activity and build up slowly. Some
conditions need a check first: poorly controlled blood pressure, untreated advanced diabetic
eye disease, nerve damage (feet or autonomic), dizziness on standing, balance problems, or a
history of foot ulcers. Tell your doctor if your exercise tolerance has dropped.

## DM-ACT-06: Low blood sugar during exercise
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, Section 5 "Hypoglycemia" under physical activity (S105)
- tags: activity, hypoglycemia, insulin, sulfonylurea, low sugar
- frequency_hint: daily
- contraindications: only for people on insulin or insulin secretagogues (e.g. sulfonylureas)

With insulin or insulin secretagogues, activity can cause low blood sugar if medicine or food
is not adjusted. Check glucose around exercise and carry a quick source of glucose; ask your
care team how to adjust.

## DM-ACT-07: Eye disease and exercise
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, Section 5 "Exercise in the Presence of Complications – Retinopathy" (S107)
- tags: retinopathy, eyes, exercise, vigorous, lifting
- frequency_hint: monthly
- contraindications: only with diabetic eye disease (retinopathy)

With proliferative or severe diabetic retinopathy, vigorous aerobic or heavy resistance
exercise may be unsafe; check with the eye doctor before intense exercise.

## DM-ACT-08: Numb feet and exercise
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, Section 5 "Peripheral Neuropathy" under exercise (S107); rec. 12.31 (footwear)
- tags: neuropathy, feet, numbness, footwear, exercise
- frequency_hint: weekly
- contraindications: only with numb or painful feet / neuropathy

Reduced feeling in the feet raises the risk of skin injury with some exercise. Wear proper
footwear, check feet after activity, and prefer low-impact activity.

## DM-GLU-01: Know your glucose goals
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 6.3a (A) and Table 6.3 (A1C < 7%, pre-meal 80–130 mg/dL, peak after meals < 180 mg/dL); recs. 6.4–6.5 and 13.7 (goals are individualised; less strict for older adults with complex health)
- tags: glucose, sugar, a1c, targets, monitoring
- frequency_hint: monthly

For many non-pregnant adults: A1C under 7%, glucose before meals 80–130 mg/dL, and under
180 mg/dL at the peak after meals. Your own goals may differ; agree them with your doctor.

## DM-GLU-02: Low blood sugar: what it is and what to do
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, Table 6.4 and text S139 (level 1: < 70 mg/dL; level 2: < 54 mg/dL, needs immediate action); rec. 6.15 (B) (treat with glucose; recheck after 15 minutes and repeat if still low; avoid high-fat/protein foods for treatment)
- tags: hypoglycemia, low sugar, glucose, shaky, sweating, treatment
- frequency_hint: daily

Below 70 mg/dL is low blood sugar; below 54 mg/dL needs immediate action. Symptoms include
shakiness, sweating, hunger, confusion and fast heartbeat. Take glucose (or another sugary
carbohydrate, not fatty or high-protein food), check again after 15 minutes and repeat if still low.

## DM-GLU-03: Check your glucose as advised
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 6.1–6.2 (assess glycemic status; A1C at least twice a year) and Section 7 (blood glucose monitoring / CGM)
- tags: glucose, monitoring, meter, cgm, a1c
- frequency_hint: daily

Check your glucose as your care team advised and keep a record; A1C is checked at least twice
a year (more often if not at goal).

## DM-FOOT-01: Check your feet every day
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 12.30 (B)
- tags: feet, foot care, neuropathy, ulcer, mirror, daily
- frequency_hint: daily

Look at and feel your feet every day (use an unbreakable mirror for the soles) to catch early
problems such as cuts, blisters, redness or swelling.

## DM-SMOKE-01: Avoid tobacco and vaping
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.40 (A)
- tags: smoking, tobacco, vape, shisha
- frequency_hint: daily
- contraindications: only if the patient smokes or vapes

Complete avoidance of tobacco and vaping is advised; counselling plus medicines helps people quit.

## DM-SLEEP-01: Healthy sleep routines
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 5.57 (A) (sleep-promoting routines) and rec. 5.56 (B) (screen for sleep problems)
- tags: sleep, night, routine, rest
- frequency_hint: daily

Keep sleep-promoting routines (regular bedtime, a calm dark room, less screen time before bed).
Sleep problems are common with diabetes; tell your care team about them.

## DM-FAST-01: Planning Ramadan or other fasting
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, recs. 5.32 (B) and 5.33 (B) (IDF-DAR pre-fasting risk assessment; adjust treatment well before fasting)
- tags: ramadan, fasting, hypoglycemia, dehydration
- frequency_hint: monthly
- contraindications: only if the patient plans to fast

Before fasting, have a risk assessment with your care team well in advance and agree how
medicines and timing should change, to avoid low or high sugar and dehydration.

## DM-BP-01: Blood pressure at home and its goal with diabetes
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, rec. 10.2 (A) (home BP monitoring), rec. 10.4 (A) (goal < 130/80 if safely attainable); 2017 ACC/AHA Section 9.6 rec. 1 (treatment goal < 130/80 in diabetes)
- tags: blood pressure, home bp, diabetes, goal, monitoring
- frequency_hint: daily

People with diabetes and high blood pressure should check BP at home after learning how; the
usual goal is under 130/80 mmHg when it can be reached safely.

## DM-PSY-01: Feeling down or overwhelmed
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, recs. 5.42–5.48 (psychosocial care; screen for diabetes distress, anxiety and depression)
- tags: mood, stress, distress, depression, anxiety
- frequency_hint: monthly

Feeling overwhelmed by diabetes, worried or low is common and treatable. Tell your care team;
they can help or refer you.

## DM-DSMES-01: Diabetes education
- source: ADA Standards of Care 2026, recs. 5.1 (A) and 5.2 (E) (diabetes self-management education and support)
- tags: education, learning, class, diabetes
- frequency_hint: monthly

Diabetes self-management education helps people make decisions and care for themselves; ask
your clinic about it, especially at diagnosis or when goals aren't met.
